## 🧬 Cross-study finding — the family-history branch fires, but does not flip the decision

**Surfaced 29 Aug 2026 by reading the [Health](README.md) and [Parents](../Parents/README.md) studies against each other.** Neither document had connected them.

The decision tree in [`decision_hdfc_vs_abhi.md`](decision_hdfc_vs_abhi.md) §4 opens with:

> *"Do you have — **or a family history of** — diabetes, hypertension, asthma or high cholesterol?"* → **YES → ABHI Activ One MAX.**

Per [`Parents/README.md`](../Parents/README.md), **both parents have BP, diabetes and high cholesterol.** On a literal reading, the buyer should be buying ABHI.

### Why the branch is over-broad

**Day-1 chronic cover only helps conditions held _at inception_.** A pre-existing disease is one diagnosed or symptomatic before the policy is issued. A condition that first arises *during* an in-force policy is a **fresh illness** — covered by any plan, with **no PED waiting period at all** (subject only to the 30-day initial wait and, for listed conditions, the specific-disease wait; diabetes and hypertension are not on those lists).

So for a healthy 26-year-old who develops diabetes at 40 under an in-force policy, **ABHI's Chronic Care buys nothing.** The branch should read *"do you have"*, not *"or a family history of"*.

> 🔧 **Framework fix:** the M2/decision-tree question must distinguish **conditions held at inception** (where day-1 chronic cover is decisive — see the [parents' inverted verdict](../Parents/hdfc_vs_abhi_father.md)) from **family history** (where it is not). Family history changes *when you should buy*, not *what*.

### What the family history _does_ change

HDFC **restarts the PED waiting periods and the 5-year moratorium on any later sum-insured increase** (M2/M6). If the buyer develops BP or diabetes in his 30s — which the family history makes likelier — then **any later SI step-up is underwritten with those as pre-existing conditions on the increment.**

> ⭐ **Consequence: whatever cover he wants at 45, he should buy now.** Topping up later is precisely the move the family history compromises. **This is a new argument for the larger sum insured, and it was not in either SI document.**

`si_decision.md` is the later and more specific analysis, and flags the divergence itself: it *"tilts lower than the earlier ₹1Cr, price-no-object call once lifelong affordability and the irreversibility of downsizing are weighted."*

**The verified numbers (age 26, Bengaluru, 0% GST):**

| SI | Age 26 | Age 60 | Day-1 cover |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| ₹25L | ₹11,073 | ₹58,472 | ₹50L |
| ₹50L | ₹12,343 | ₹74,933 | ₹1Cr |
| ₹1Cr | ₹13,535 | ₹83,205 | ₹2Cr |

**The tension in one line:** going big is cheap *today* (₹1Cr is only 1.41× the ₹10L premium at 26) but **materially dearer at 60** (2.05×) — against which the **waiting-period reset on any increase**, now sharpened by the family-history finding above, argues for buying the target cover immediately.

> **₹50L is the tier that absorbs both arguments** — ₹1Cr usable from day one, +₹1,270/yr over ₹25L today, +₹16,461/yr at 60. **Not yet decided — this is the primary question for the advisor calls** (see [`advisor_second_opinion.md`](advisor_second_opinion.md)).

## 📋 Before payment — the schedule checklist

Everything here lives on the **policy schedule**, not the wording, which means the **issuing channel** controls it. See [`purchase_channel.md`](purchase_channel.md).

- [ ] Schedule names **"Optima Secure `+`"** — cheaper capped-room siblings share the same UIN
- [ ] **§2.13 room-rent modifier is NOT applied** — it re-enables proportionate deduction, the single trap this pick exists to avoid
- [ ] **Annual premium, not instalments** — M6's instalment trap: claim while on EMI and the remaining instalments are netted off the payout
- [ ] **§2.12 PED-waiting reduction** — channel-level only; ask whether the channel can attach it (expect no)
- [ ] **Pin-code-level cashless list** for the buyer's Bengaluru neighbourhood — the 620 figure is PolicyBazaar's; other sources give 378/360
