HDFC ERGO Optima Secure+ · SI decision

Which sum insured — ₹10L, ₹25L, ₹50L or ₹1 Crore?

A deep comparison of the four SI tiers on one plan — HDFC Optima Secure+ — for a healthy 26-year-old, on verified PolicyBazaar quotes at four ages (26/40/50/60). Two facts drive everything: its Secure Benefit doubles cover on day 1 and its uncapped Infinite Benefit adds 100% of base every year — so the base mostly buys early-year cover. And the premium is cheap to go big at 26, but the gap widens sharply with age.

The decisive graph — effective cover over time

This is exact, computed from the benefit rules (not premium): usable cover = base × (year + 1) — because Secure adds 100% on day one and Infinite adds another 100% of base every completed year, uncapped and claim-proof. Watch how the base you pick sets the whole trajectory.

● exact — from Secure + Infinite benefit rules
The reframing. Because Infinite is uncapped, the base mainly decides your day-one and early-year cover — exactly the window before the bonus has accrued. So the real question isn't "how much cover will I ever have" (all tiers grow huge); it's "how big a claim could I face in the first 2–3 years, before Infinite builds up?" ₹25L gives ₹50L day-one — enough for any single catastrophe — then compounds to ₹2.75Cr in a decade. That's the efficient answer; ₹50L adds a ₹1Cr day-one cushion.

04

The premium over your lifetime — what you're really committing to

Verified quotes at four ages (26/40/50/60). Going big is nearly free at 26 — but that's a young-age effect. Premium barely moves to 40 (~1.3%/yr), then climbs ~6.7%/yr in the 40s and ~10%/yr in the 50s (plus ~6%/yr medical-inflation repricing on top). The tiers fan apart with age, so the base you pick is a lifelong commitment.

Annual premium vs age, by SI tier (₹)

Flat and bunched to 40; then the J-curve, and the bigger tiers pull away. The gap you pay for a big base is small at 26 and large at 60.

Per lakh, bigger is still cheaper at every age (₹1Cr = ₹135/lakh @26, ₹832 @60) — so a big base is efficient. But the absolute premium gap versus a small base is back-loaded onto the expensive decades: ₹50L costs +₹2,774/yr over ₹10L at 26, but +₹34,248/yr at 60. Since Infinite grows any base to crores anyway, that escalating premium mostly buys day-one cover — which is why the efficient choice is a moderate base, not a maximal one. (One step stays cheap at any age: ₹50L→₹1Cr, ~11%.)

Why this tilts below the earlier "₹1Cr, price no object" call: at 26 going big looked nearly free, but the verified 4-age curve shows the gap widening sharply after 50, and reducing SI later forfeits accumulated Infinite Benefit (§2.17.v) — so the base is a lifelong, one-way commitment. Size it to a premium you can carry for 40 years. If affordability ever bites, don't cut SI — use the Aggregate Deductible discount (keeps SI + bonus), a super top-up, or multi-year pre-payment.
