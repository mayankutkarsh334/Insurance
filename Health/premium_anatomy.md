## The three laws of the premium

| | Law | The number | Meaning |
|---|-----|-----------|---------|
| **1** | **Age** — cheap decade, then a J-curve | **1 → ~10×** | What ₹1 costs at 26 costs ~3× at 55, ~10× at 75. Near-flat in the 20s–30s; the cliff is after 60 (+40%/band). |
| **2** | **Sum insured** — bigger cover, cheaper per rupee | **₹1,763 → ₹355 / lakh** | Premium is *sub-linear* in SI. Doubling cover adds ~25–35%, not 100%. A large SI bought young is doubly rewarded (cheaper/lakh **and** a flatter age-curve). |
| **3** | **You** — features add, health can multiply | **up to ×2.5** | Riders/SI nudge the base; a declared health condition can **load the premium +100–150%**, permanently. Claim-based renewal loading is banned everywhere. |

## 3b. Add inflation — your premium over the next 30 years

The age tables above are in **today's rupees**. They understate the future because the insurer also **re-prices the whole product** every few years to chase **medical inflation** (India's runs ~12–14%/yr — the highest in Asia). Real bill = **age-band steps × cumulative re-pricing**.

**Worked example — SBI Platinum ₹25L bought at 26 (verified base ₹22,727), projected to age 56:**

| At age (yrs held) | Age-only (today's ₹) | +6%/yr | +8%/yr | +10%/yr |
|-------------------|---------------------:|-------:|-------:|--------:|
| 26 (entry) | 22,727 | 22,727 | 22,727 | 22,727 |
| 36 (+10) | 25,431 | 45,550 | 54,910 | 65,970 |
| 46 (+20) | 31,331 | 1,00,500 | 1,46,000 | 2,10,800 |
| **56 (+30)** | **44,558** | **2,55,900** | **4,48,200** | **7,77,500** |

> Age-banding is **verified** from the rate chart; the re-pricing rate is an **assumption**, bracketed 6/8/10%. **8% is not hypothetical for everyone — Bajaj contractually prints +8%/yr.**

**Nominal vs real — the honest read.** Much of that ₹2.5–7.8L is money being worth less. Discount back at ~5% general inflation and the age-56 premium is really **≈₹59k (6%) / ≈₹1.04L (8%) / ≈₹1.80L (10%) in today's money** — a **1.3× to 4× real rise** over what a 56-year-old pays today. The part *above* general inflation **is** medical inflation, and it's the real squeeze.

**Three defences, all set at age 26:**
1. A plan with **no contractual escalator** (rules out Bajaj's +8%/yr).
2. A **large SI** — its flatter age-curve + no-claim bonus absorb inflation.
3. **Never lapse** — then re-pricing is the *only* lever that can touch your premium, never your health.

## 3c. When your premium rises — how you find out & how it's decided

**You always see the new number before you pay** — an increase is never silently auto-charged. Two channels:
1. **The renewal notice** (~30 days before expiry, email/SMS/app) states the exact premium for the coming year.
2. **A separate advance notice for a product-wide rate revision** — the wordings require **prior IRDAI approval + written notice before it takes effect.** Notice period differs:

| Plan | Advance notice before a rate revision |
|------|---------------------------------------|
| SBI (§G.n), Bajaj (§14), ACKO (§5.1.16) | **3 months** |
| Aditya Birla MAX (E.1.4) | **30 days** (weaker) |

**The increment is built from three separable layers** — and you can audit which one moved:

| Layer | What it is | Can it be aimed at you personally? |
|-------|-----------|-----------------------------------|
| **1. Age-band step** | Crossing into a new 5-year band → the next band's rate on the published chart | No — same for everyone your age; look it up |
| **2. Portfolio / block re-pricing** | Insurer revises the base rate for the *whole product* for medical inflation + book claims experience | **No** — needs prior IRDAI approval, applies to the whole cohort, and **cannot** be based on your individual claims (*"no loading shall apply on renewals based on individual claims experience"*). Bajaj is the exception that pre-prints +8%/yr |
| **3. Things you changed** | SI enhancement, zone move, adding/dropping a rider or discount | Yes, but only because you acted. A one-time health **loading** is set only at entry / SI-enhancement / porting — never sprung on a normal renewal |

**How to check your own hike:** did I cross an age band? was there an IRDAI-approved product-wide revision (the 3-month notice tells you, and peers your age see the same)? did I change SI/zone/riders? If none explain it, ask for the break-up — and they legally **cannot** have raised it because you claimed. This is why a **published rate chart matters**: SBI and Bajaj publish one, and HDFC's tiers are now verified from quotes — you have a baseline to audit against. **ACKO's pricing is algorithmic with no chart, so its increment can't be independently audited** — the one real weakness on this dimension.

## 4. How premium moves with your health (underwriting loading)

Base premium assumes a *standard healthy* life. A declared condition can attach a **percentage loading** on top of the chart — usually **permanent, applied at entry and every renewal**. It stacks on age and SI.

| Plan | Stated max loading | Character |
|------|--------------------|-----------|
| **HDFC Optima Secure+** | **+100% / condition · +150% / person** ✓ cap | Only plan publishing a ceiling on *both* axes |
| **SBI Super Health** | **+150% / person** ✓ cap · **lifelong** | Persists from inception through all renewals |
| Aditya Birla MAX | *not published* | Loading / extra waiting / exclusion via counter-offer |
| Bajaj Health Guard | *not published* | Loading possible at entry; no medicals to 45 |
| Care Supreme | *not published* | **No medical tests to 65** → usually no result to load on |
| ACKO Platinum | *no stated %* | Prices risk via **0–3 yr PED waiting + exclusions**, not a % surcharge |

**Two honest reads:**
1. A **stated cap is better than silence** — Bajaj/ABHI/Care/ACKO leave the maximum open-ended.
2. For a **healthy 26-year-old the expected loading is zero** on every plan — which is precisely why this table argues for buying **now**: a condition diagnosed at 40 can attach +50–150% that rides the premium for life. The lever is **health disclosed at entry**, never claim history (claim-based renewal loading is banned industry-wide).

## 4b. If you fall ill mid-policy — disclosure & re-rating

**The question that decides a 40-year hold.** On a **continuously renewed** policy you do **not** re-fill a proposal form each year, and a disease you develop *while covered* **cannot** re-rate you — it's a **claim**, not a new disclosure. Disclosure only bites when you **re-underwrite**.

| Situation | Re-underwritten? | Disclose the new illness? | Premium rise *for your health*? |
|-----------|------------------|---------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Normal on-time renewal, same policy | No — no fresh proposal | No | **No** — only the standard age-band + cohort re-pricing everyone gets |
| A disease you develop *while covered* | No | No — it's a claim | **No** — fully covered; renewal can't be denied for claiming or ageing |
| You **increase** the Sum Insured | Yes — on the increment | Yes, for the top-up slice | **Yes on the increase** — waiting clocks & moratorium restart on the added SI; condition can be loaded/excluded on that slice |
| You **port** to a new insurer | Yes — fully | Yes — full fresh proposal | **Yes** — new insurer underwrites you fresh and can load/exclude the disease you now have |
| Renew **after a lapse/break** | May be treated as fresh | Possibly | Possibly — avoid lapses; use the 30-day grace |

> **The 60-month moratorium — your permanent shield.** Every plan in this study carries it: after **5 continuous years**, the insurer can no longer contest the policy or a claim on non-disclosure or PED grounds — **only proven fraud** survives (plus exclusions already named in your schedule). The disclosure risk you carry from day one **expires at year 5 and never returns**, as long as you keep renewing.

**This is the single biggest reason to buy young and never lapse.** A diabetes/heart diagnosis at 40 is fully covered and cannot re-rate your existing policy — but shopping *fresh* at 40 with that diagnosis means a **+50–150% loading, a fresh PED waiting period, or a permanent exclusion** at entry. Continuous cover converts "insurable at 26" into "insured for life."

_Verified: all seven wordings state "no loading shall apply on renewals based on individual claims experience" + lifelong renewability. Re-underwriting triggers (SI-enhancement clock resets, porting) from the SBI / Care / Aditya Birla Module-2 findings. Always disclose fully and in writing at the moments marked "Yes"._

## 6. What to buy at each age

The through-line: **buy a large SI young** — cheaper per lakh, flatter curve, and it banks waiting-period credit before any condition can attach a loading.

| Age | Cover to hold | Features that earn their keep |
|-----|---------------|-------------------------------|
| **20s (25–30) · buy now** | **SI ₹25L–₹1Cr** | Uncapped bonus (Infinite), unlimited restore, no room-rent cap, no-cost EMI. Skip wellness gimmicks & PED features you don't need. |
| **30s (30–40) · consolidate** | **SI ₹50L–₹1Cr** | Raise SI *before* health drifts (an SI hike won't load yet). Consumables buy-back, maternity (if planned), wellness earn-back. |
| **40s (40–50) · last easy window** | **SI ₹1Cr, locked** | Bands step +12–23%; a new condition here loads *permanently*. PED-waiting reductions now matter. Prefer **no contractual escalator**. |
| **50s (50–60) · manage the cliff** | Base + super top-up | Max out accrued no-claim bonus (free cover). A **flatter-curve / zone-agnostic** plan (SBI Infinite) ages most gently. Deductible discount. |
| **60+ · expensive years** | Never lapse | Value shifts to **OPD, domiciliary, day-care, consumables** and **zero co-pay**. Guaranteed lifelong renewability (all 7 have it) is now the whole point. |

## 7. Will my cover keep pace with medical inflation?

**The problem you must design against:** a *fixed* SI is a *shrinking* SI. At ~12–14% medical inflation, **₹25L today buys only ~₹7–8L of treatment in 10 years.** Cover has to grow. Four levers do that:

1. **No-claim / cumulative bonus — the automatic engine.** Each claim-free year adds a % of base SI. The main hedge. What matters: rate, ceiling, and whether a claim resets it.
2. **Instant multiplier (2× / Secure).** Doubles base from day 1 (HDFC Secure, ABHI 2X).
3. **A bigger base SI at entry** — cleanest hedge (premium is sub-linear in SI; the curve is flatter at high SI).
4. **A super top-up added later** (40–45) to lift total cover cheaply once the base engine plateaus.

**Bonus engine, plan by plan (₹25L base, claim-free):**

| Plan | Engine | Ceiling on ₹25L | Claim-proof? | Keeps pace with 14% for ~ |
|------|--------|-----------------|--------------|---------------------------|
| **HDFC Optima Secure+** | +100%/yr **uncapped** + 2× Secure | **no ceiling** ⭐ | ✅ | **forever** |
| **Aditya Birla MAX** | +100%/yr → 6× | ₹1.5Cr (cap ₹3Cr) | ✅ | ~14 years |
| **SBI Platinum** | Enhanced CB → +200% | ₹75L | ❌ shrinks on claim | ~8 years |
| **Bajaj** | +150% (50/50/10×5) | ₹62.5L | ❌ drops a slab on claim | ~7 years |
| **Care Supreme** | +50%/yr → 100% (base) | ₹50L (₹1.5Cr paid add-on) | ✅ | ~5 years |
| **ACKO Platinum** | +10%/yr → 100% | ₹50L | ⚠️ only if "Inflation Protect", not "NCB" | ~5 years |

**Honest reality check:**
- **Only an uncapped, claim-proof engine truly keeps pace forever** — HDFC's Infinite Benefit. A large-cap engine (ABHI 6×) lasts ~14 years; **capped engines (SBI/Bajaj/Care/ACKO) plateau after 5–8 years**, then the ₹25L quietly falls behind again.
- **Every bonus except the claim-proof ones shrinks or resets when you claim** — right when you've proven you need cover.
- **Restore / recharge is NOT inflation growth** — it refills the pot *within a year* (protects against a second illness), it doesn't grow cover year-on-year.

**So the robust combination (buying at 26):** a large base SI now + an uncapped claim-proof bonus + a super top-up held in reserve. That keeps real cover ahead of medical inflation for the full 40-year hold rather than letting ₹25L erode to ~₹7L of real cover.

## 8. How to increase your SI over time

Five routes, ranked frictionless → disruptive. **The key fact: actively raising the base SI re-underwrites the *increase*** — the added slice gets fresh waiting periods, a restarted moratorium, and possible loading if you've since developed a condition (SBI: *"enhancing the SI restarts all three clocks on the increase"*; Care: *"SI-enhancement restarts every waiting clock and the moratorium on the increase"*). **The cheapest, cleanest cover you'll ever buy is the one you buy today.**

| Route | How it works | Friction | Best used |
|-------|-------------|----------|-----------|
| **1. Bonus engine (auto)** | Cumulative bonus adds % of base each claim-free year | **None** | Always on; pick uncapped + claim-proof |
| **2. Inflation-protect rider** | Auto-bumps SI (e.g. ACKO Inflation Protect +10%/yr) | None if built-in | Turn on at entry if offered |
| **3. Enhance base SI at renewal** | Request higher SI (₹25L→₹50L) | ⚠️ increment re-underwritten; fresh waits + loading risk; often only to ~age 60–65 | Early & while healthy |
| **4. Add a super top-up** | 2nd policy, deductible = base SI; only pays after base exhausts, so cheap | Low — own fresh waits, mild coordination | Reach ₹1Cr+ at 40–45 without re-pricing the aged base |
| **5. Port to a bigger-SI plan** | Switch insurer, carry waiting-period credit | ⚠️ high — new insurer underwrites the increment, can load your new condition | Only to fix a deficient plan |

**Recommended sequence (buying at 26):**
1. **Buy big now** (₹50L–₹1Cr) so you rarely need route 3 — cheap per lakh, and it avoids every waiting-period reset later.
2. **Let bonus + inflation rider run** (routes 1–2) — free, frictionless growth.
3. **If you must step up, do it early** (route 3, in your 30s) so the increment attracts no loading.
4. **Reserve a super top-up** (route 4) for your 40s when the base engine plateaus.
5. **Keep porting (route 5) in reserve** only to escape a bad plan.

**Plan SI headroom:** Aditya Birla MAX up to **₹6Cr** (most room); HDFC up to ₹2Cr (but uncapped Infinite may make enhancement unnecessary); SBI / Bajaj / Care / ACKO ~₹1Cr base → top-ups become the main lever there.
