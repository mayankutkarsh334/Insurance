# 🕵️ Blind Second Opinion — advisor call plan

_Designed 29 August 2026 · for the [HDFC Optima Secure+ decision](decision_record.md) · channel background in [purchase_channel.md](purchase_channel.md)_

> **Why do this at all?** The [plan choice](decision_hdfc_vs_abhi.md) is the most-researched decision in this repo; an advisor is unlikely to out-argue it. **The sum insured is the least-researched, and the two SI documents contradict each other.** So the plan question is *the test*; the SI question is *the value*.
>
> **The design:** approach two differently-biased channels — [Ditto](purchase_channel.md) (corporate agent, salaried advisors, reportedly HDFC-heavy) and PolicyBazaar (broker, commission-driven, in-person) — give each the identical profile without naming a shortlist, and see whether they converge. **Agreement from Ditto specifically is weak evidence**, which is why one opinion isn't enough.

---

## 📞 Ditto — two-call plan

**Scope: your own individual cover only.** Your parents' policies are a separate decision, already made — keep them out of this meeting except as family history.

**Three rules:** don't name a plan · don't name your shortlist · don't buy on the call.

If asked whether you've researched already:
> *"I've done some reading, but I'd rather not say what — I want your independent take without anchoring you."*

True, non-leading, and an advisor who knows you're informed will engage more substantively than one who thinks you're a beginner.

---

## 0. The two-call structure

Follow-up consultations are free and rescheduling works over WhatsApp — so **don't cram this into one session.**

| | **Call 1 — BLIND** | **Call 2 — INFORMED** |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | An unbiased read you can test your study against | Depth on what's actually unresolved |
| You reveal | Nothing | Everything — shortlist, numbers, clause references |
| Covers | §2 opening · §3 Tier-1 five questions | §10 agenda |
| Constraint | **One shot. The blind test doesn't survive a retry** | None — take as long as you need |

**Between the two:** email the Tier-3 questions (§3c), score them (§8), and check every factual claim they made against your module files.

**Ask for the same advisor on call 2** — continuity matters if you eventually buy through them.

> ⚠️ **Don't book call 1 until you're genuinely prepared.** It's the only part of this that can't be redone.

## ⏱️ Call 1 — the time budget

| Min | Phase | Who leads | Your job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0–3** | Your opening dump (§2) | **You** | Front-load every fact so they don't spend 8 min extracting it |
| **3–6** | Their follow-up intake | Them | Answer fast, don't elaborate |
| **6–15** | Their recommendation + reasoning | Them | **Shut up and take notes.** Do not interrupt or challenge |
| **15–26** | Your Tier-1 questions (§3) | **You** | Five questions. That's all that fits |
| **26–30** | The close (§6) | **You** | Secure the written follow-up |

> ⚠️ **You have room for ~5 real questions, not 10.** Everything else goes to email afterwards. Trying to fit all ten means asking all of them badly.

## 1. Have ready before you dial

**Your facts** (they'll ask — knowing them cold buys you minutes):
- DOB · Bengaluru pincode · Gaya pincode **823001**
- **Height & weight** (they need BMI for underwriting)
- Smoking / alcohol status
- Rough annual income bracket
- Your conditions: **none**
- **Parents: both have BP, diabetes, high cholesterol, fatty liver**

**Your setup** (it's a video call):
- This doc open on a **second screen or your phone** — not the same window as the call
- Blank doc for live notes, or paper
- Headphones, quiet room, decent light
- Ask at the start: *"Mind if I take notes / record this?"*

**⚠️ Do not quote premium figures from your own study.** Naming "₹12,343 for ₹50L" identifies the plan instantly and blows the blind test.

**Do not give:** PAN, Aadhaar, or any payment detail. None of it is needed for advice.

## 2. The 3-minute opening — say this, then stop

> "I'm 26, healthy, no existing conditions, non-smoker. I work in **Bengaluru** but my home town is **Gaya in Bihar** — I visit often and could realistically be hospitalised there or in **Patna**.
>
> **Both my parents have diabetes, high blood pressure and high cholesterol — I mention that only as family history. Their cover is handled separately; today I'm asking purely about my own.**
>
> I want an individual policy just for myself — not a floater — that I'll hold and renew for the rest of my life. **Budget is flexible; I care far more about whether they actually pay a claim than about the premium.**
>
> I haven't decided on anything. What would you recommend, and why?"

Then **stop talking.** Let them work. The silence is doing your job for you.

---

## 3. Tier 1 — the five that must be asked (minutes 15–26)

| # | Question | What it's really testing |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **"What sum insured should I buy — and should I buy it now or step up later?"** | ⭐ **Your highest-value question.** See §4 |
| **2** | **"Both my parents are diabetic and hypertensive. For *my own* policy, does that family history change what I should buy?"** | ⭐ The trap. See §4 |
| **3** | **"What's the strongest argument *against* your own recommendation?"** | Advisor vs salesperson. The single most revealing thing you can ask |
| **4** | "Does it have a room-rent cap or any co-pay? What are the waiting periods?" | Whether they lead with the traps or hide them |
| **5** | "Which two or three did you consider and reject, and why?" | Whether they compared at all, or reached for a house favourite |

## 3b. Tier 2 — only if the clock allows

- "How many cashless hospitals does it have in **Bengaluru**? In **Patna** and **Gaya**?"
- "How does Ditto get paid on this, and does the commission differ by insurer?" *(they're legally required to disclose)*

## 3c. Tier 3 — send by email, don't burn call time

- The **§2.12 probe**: *"On HDFC Optima Secure+, clause 2.12 lets the PED waiting period be cut to 24 or 12 months but only at channel level. Can you attach that?"* — better by email anyway, since they'll need to check. Watch whether the answer is honest or a bluff.
- Written recommendation + reasoning
- Whether any schedule-level modifiers (room-rent caps, voluntary co-pay) get applied by their channel

## 4. The two answers to listen to hardest

### ⭐ Q1 — Sum insured. This is what you're actually buying the 30 minutes for.

A **strong** answer touches:
- The premium curve **steepens sharply after 50** — affordability is a post-50 problem, not a today problem
- **Raising SI later restarts the waiting periods and the moratorium on the increment** — this is the real argument for buying big now
- Bonus / multiplier mechanics grow your cover annually anyway, so day-one SI isn't the whole picture
- **Super top-up** as a cheap later lever instead of enlarging the base

A **weak** answer: *"₹10L is fine, you're young."* That ignores metro bill inflation and every mechanic above.

### ⭐ Q2 — Family history. Most advisors get this wrong.

A **weak** answer: *"Yes — buy a plan with day-1 chronic cover."* **That's wrong for you.** Day-1 chronic cover only helps conditions you already have *at inception*. If you develop diabetes at 40 under an in-force policy, it's a fresh illness with no PED wait, on any plan.

A **strong** answer: family history matters because it argues for **buying your full sum insured now** — since any later SI increase is underwritten afresh, with anything you've developed by then treated as pre-existing on the increment.

If they give the strong answer unprompted, you're talking to someone good. Weight everything else they said upward.

---

## 5. Live red-flag checklist

- ☐ Recommended before asking which city you live in
- ☐ Pushed **instalment / EMI premium** 🚩
- ☐ Suggested something with a **co-pay** or **room-rent cap** despite a flexible budget
- ☐ Named one insurer immediately, no alternatives considered
- ☐ Tried to close in the room
- ☐ Bluffed on a factual question rather than saying "I'll check"
- ☐ **Pivoted to selling cover for your parents** 🚩 — politely refuse and steer back; this is the most likely way you lose the 30 minutes

---

## 6. The close — last 3 minutes, don't skip

1. **"Can you email me the recommendation and the reasoning?"** ← non-negotiable
2. "What's your commission on this, and does it differ between insurers?"
3. "If I come back in a week, do I speak to you or someone else?" *(continuity matters for claim-time support)*
4. **"I'm gathering opinions this week — I won't decide today."**

## 9. What a successful call looks like

**Not** them agreeing with you. Ditto is reportedly HDFC-ERGO-heavy, so agreement is weak evidence either way.

**Success is one of three things:**
1. A **strong SI answer** that resolves the ₹25L vs ₹50L vs ₹1Cr question your own two documents disagree on
2. A **specific factual claim your study can't rebut** — a genuine finding
3. Them pushing **ICICI Elevate** or **Niva Bupa ReAssure** with real reasoning — both are on their panel, both are named reserves in your notes, and **neither was ever deep-studied.** That's your blind spot, not their error

## 10. Call 2 — the informed deep-dive

Open by dropping the constraint: *"I've shortlisted HDFC ERGO Optima Secure+ and Aditya Birla Activ One MAX. I want to pressure-test that with you."*

### A. The decision you actually need help with

- **"₹25L vs ₹50L vs ₹1Cr — which, and why?"** Now you *can* quote your own figures: **₹11,073 / ₹12,343 / ₹13,535 at 26**, rising to **₹58,472 / ₹74,933 / ₹83,205 at 60.**
- "Does the premium curve after 50 argue for buying smaller now, or does the waiting-period reset on any SI increase argue for buying big?"
- "Where would a **super top-up** fit later, versus enlarging the base now?"

### B. Clause-level checks before purchase

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| **§2.12** — can your channel attach the PED-waiting reduction (36 → 24/12 mo)? | Channel-level only; you can't opt for it yourself |
| **§2.13** — confirm the voluntary room-rent modifier will **not** be applied | It re-enables proportionate deduction — the trap the whole pick avoids |
| Schedule must name **"Optima Secure +"** | Cheaper capped-room siblings share the UIN |
| Confirm **annual premium, not instalments** | Claiming while on EMI nets remaining instalments off the payout |
| **Bengaluru pincode-level** cashless list for your neighbourhood | Your 620 figure is one source; others say 378/360 |

### C. Make them argue against you

- "HDFC's solvency dipped to **1.56×** in Jun-2024 and its combined ratio is ~123% and worsening. Does that worry you?"
- "It's the only one of my finalists with a **claims-experience discount you lose by claiming** — 21%/18%. How much should that weigh?"
- **"Make the case for ICICI Elevate and Niva Bupa ReAssure."** ⭐ Both are on their panel; **neither was ever deep-studied in your file.** This is the single most likely place they add something you don't have.

### D. If you're leaning toward buying through them

- "What do you actually do for me at claim time?"
- "What's your commission on this, and does it differ between insurers?"
- "What should I check on the schedule during the 15-day free-look?"
